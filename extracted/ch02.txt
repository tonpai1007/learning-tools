Metal treatment
• Annealing
– Process of heating the metal for a certain time above its critical temperature,
then cooling down slowly in the furnace
– During heated, carbon is diffused throughout the metal.
– After heat-treated, the metal returns to its equilibrium state
– Advantages
• Ductility ↑ (Residual stress released)
• Strength ↑
Sources: https://makeitfrommetal.com/beginners-guide-on-how-to-anneal-steel/

Metal treatment
• Normalising
– Process of heating the metal for a certain time above its critical temperature,
then taken out to cool down in atmosphere
– During heated, carbon is diffused throughout the metal.
– After heat-treated, the metal returns to its equilibrium state
– Advantages
• Ductility ↑ (Residual stress released)
• Strength ↑
• Finer grain than annealing

Metal treatment
• Quenching
– Process of heating the metal for a certain time above its critical temperature,
then taken out to cool down immediately in a quenching medium such as water,
oil, or other inorganic salts or organic aqueous solutions
− Oil quenching: Slow, can prevent
crack caused by rapid expansion
− Water quenching: Quicker, used for
carbon steels, medium-carbon, low-
alloy steel
− Air quenching: Called mild quenching
(similar to normalising)
– Advantages
• Hardness ↑ (Metal structure changed)
• Brittleness ↑
• Ability to withstand wear and vibration
